## Why these terms

Standard [CC BY-NC 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/legalcode.en) restricts purposes directed toward commercial advantage; it does not automatically allow a qualifying startup's business use. CC also [discourages using its licenses for software](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#can-i-apply-a-creative-commons-license-to-software). A split CC/software scheme would not express the same approved business boundary across this kit.

[PolyForm Small Business 1.0.0](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/small-business/1.0.0) uses an employee-and-contractor limit **and** a revenue limit, not the approved employee **or** valuation test. [PolyForm Internal Use 1.0.0](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/internal-use/1.0.0) permits internal business use without those eligibility limits. [PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0) does not supply the approved startup exception. None is relabeled or modified here.

The [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd) does not permit restrictions on business fields of use. Blitz therefore describes the new terms as source-available. The custom license states the continuing rights for compliant recipients. Existing [Creative Commons grants are irrevocable](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#what-if-i-change-my-mind-about-using-a-cc-license), and a new repository notice cannot retract them.

These terms express the owner's stated policy; they are not a legal opinion on enforceability, title or every possible use. No governing law, dispute venue or third-party patent clearance is asserted. Borderline startup status, disputed valuation or uses combining internal work with client services can be addressed in a written permission request.
